## What is a self-completion questionnaire?
- Definition: Self-completion questionnaires are survey tools completed by respondents without interviewer assistance
- Also called self-administrated and postal questionnaire
- Respondent writes answers on form
- No interviewer present
- Therefore, anonymity encourages more honest and reflective responses
- Returned to researcher or deposited for collection

<!-- slide-break -->
## Types of self-completion questionnaires - (1) Paper-based
- Paper-based self-completion questionnaires
- Description: Traditional method, consists of printed questions distributed physically.
- Usage: Used in settings where internet access is limited or for specific demographic groups less familiar with digital tools.
- Advantages: Tangible, can feel more personal; easier for some demographics.
- Challenges: Higher cost and time for distribution and data entry.

<!-- slide-break -->
## Types of self-completion questionnaires - (2) Mobile-based
- Mobile-based self-completion questionnaires
- Description: Surveys optimized for mobile devices, accessible through apps or mobile-friendly websites.
- Usage: Increasingly popular due to the widespread use of smartphones.
- Advantages: Convenience for respondents, real-time data collection, geolocation features.
- Challenges: Screen size limitations; need for mobile optimization.

<!-- slide-break -->
## Types of self-completion questionnaires - (3) Online-based
- Online-based self-completion questionnaires
- Description: Digital questionnaires distributed via email, social media, or dedicated platforms (e.g., Qualtrics, Google Forms).
- Usage: Ideal for reaching a broad audience quickly and cost-effectively.
- Advantages: Automated data collection and analysis; easy to edit and distribute.
- Challenges: Requires internet access; potential for lower response rates due to survey fatigue.

<!-- slide-break -->
## Comparing self-completion questionnaires and face to face structured surveys
- Method of Administration: Self administered vs. interviewer administered.
- Respondent Engagement: Varies from passive (self-completion) to active (structured). Self-completion provides convenience for respondents.
- Data Depth and Quality: Structured surveys offer more control but may introduce interviewer effect; self-completion allows for more candid responses but can suffer from lower engagement. No interviewer variability and absence of interviewer effects.
- Suitability: Choice depends on research objectives, target population, available resources, and the nature of information sought.

<!-- slide-break -->
## Interviewer effect
- Characteristics of interviewers (and respondents) may affect the answers that people give.
- Characteristics such as age, gender, race, ethnicity, and the social background of interviewers may combine to bias the answers that respondents provide.
- Since there is no interviewer present when a self-completion questionnaire is being completed, interviewer effects are eliminated.
- People are more likely to exhibit social desirability bias when an interviewer is present.
- The interviewer effect (also called interviewer variance or interviewer error) is the distortion of response to a personal interview which results from differential reactions to the social style and personality of interviewers or to their presentation of particular questions.

<!-- slide-break -->
## Interviewer effect - Impacts on research and minimizing interviewer effect
### Impacts on research
- Response Bias: Respondents may alter their answers to please the interviewer or avoid judgment.
- Social Desirability Bias: Tendency to give socially acceptable answers rather than truthful ones.
- Consistency and Comparability Issues: Variations in interviewer effect can lead to inconsistencies in responses across different interviews.

### Minimizing interviewer effect
- Training: Thorough training of interviewers to maintain neutrality and consistency.
- Standardization: Using structured interview protocols to limit variation in question delivery.
- Two researchers: Using two researchers for control.
- Two researchers: Some level of interviewer effect is inevitable.

## Disadvantages of self-completion questionnaires
- Cannot probe or prompt
- Can only ask salient questions
- No complex questions
- Respondent can see the whole questionnaire before answering (question order effects)
- Cannot ensure that the ‘right’ person answers
- Respondent fatigue if too many questions
- Excludes people with limited literacy skills
- Greater risk of missing data
- Lower response rates

<!-- slide-break -->
## Tips to improve response rates to self-completion questionnaires
- Since response rates are relatively low there is a risk of sample bias; a rate of 60%+ is needed to be acceptable.
- Response rates can be improved by:
    - Writing a good covering letter
    - Including a stamped addressed envelope
    - Issuing reminders
    - Using shorter questionnaires
    - Give clear instructions
    - Using a layout attractive to the respondent
    - Use few open-ended questions (actually, don’t)
    - Providing monetary incentives

## Arrange fixed answers vertically
- Because this makes the questionnaire less confusing to read.
- Because questions can be distinguished from answers.
- Because the respondent is less likely to make a mistake.
- Because the questionnaire is easier for data entry.
